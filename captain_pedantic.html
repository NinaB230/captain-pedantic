Captain Pedantic

Del's rundown of the ops processes that keep us running — and why the details genuinely matter.

Del Carlyle-Hoggan Internal Ops GETPEDANTIC

Following the glamour of Glenfiddich whiskey stunts in New York, Del took to the floor to remind us all of something important: we are, every one of us, ops.

The Brief

There's a superhero standing on a Pallet of Truth in our warehouse. He goes by Captain Pedantic. His shield says "We Are All Ops." And his message, delivered by Del with characteristic calm and dry wit, is this: the processes exist for a reason, and when we all follow them, everyone's job gets easier.

Del walked us through the GETPEDANTIC framework — an acrostic covering everything from the Goods Register to Client Storage — with the same energy you'd use to explain something that genuinely matters to people you like. Which is exactly what it was.

"After the glamour of Glenfiddich, this is going to be very dry nuts and bolts. But it falls to me to do the pedantry sometimes of what we perhaps should and shouldn't be doing."

GETPEDANTIC
G
Goods Register
Fill it in, in full — and mark it complete when it's done. Use Collection Notes too. Knowing what's in a box, where it came from, and where it's going is what keeps HQ running smoothly. Open the Goods Register →
E
Everything in its Place
Use the correct bay. Label everything with job number, name, and contents. A few seconds of labelling saves everyone else a lot of hunting around.
T
Truck Bookings
Use the calendar to book the truck. Fill up the fuel. Keep it tidy. Three things. That's it. Bookings go through Elle Banyard, Office Manager — she's your first port of call.
P
Parking
Don't park like a Pretty Bad Parking Person. And never — under any circumstances — in front of the shutter.
E
Equipment
Book sessions through Rentman, manage usage on site, and think carefully about repatriation. Getting kit back in good order saves the ops team hours of thankless work.
D
Derig
The derig always gets forgotten — but it's just as important as the build. Think materials, resource, logistics, warehousing, and disposal. Talk to the team before things arrive back at the warehouse so there are people, forklifts, and space ready. Don't Derig Like a Dipsomaniac.
A
Ask
If you're in doubt about any of this — kit, process, support — just ask.
N
Notify
Information is king. Keep subject headings accurate. File locations consistent. Use the ops@ email. And please — don't let an email chain drift so far from its original topic that nobody knows what it's about. Del has feelings about this.
T
Talk
When you need the ops team on site, call them. They are genuinely not always at their desks. Radios exist for a reason.
I
Incoming
Deliveries need planning. Think space management, labelling, out-of-office cover, forklifts, and load buddies. Don't just let things turn up and hope for the best.
C
Client Storage
Use the template. Take on-site photos with dimensions. Think warehouse logistics. The dream: client goods come back, we photograph them, store them properly, no extra cost. It's achievable.
